    Cassette Bias Calibration

    For 2-head and 3-head decks that have internal bias trim pots, or an external bias control. Record a low tone and a 10 kHz tone onto a blank tape, play it back, and compare their levels. Too much bias rolls off the highs. Too little makes them bright and distorted.

    1. Test tone to record

    Plays the low and high tones together at equal level. Connect your computer's output to the deck's line in and record it onto a blank tape. Keep the level low, since a loud 10 kHz tone saturates the tape and gives false readings.

    Choose the sound device connected to your cassette deck's line input. The test tones will play through this output.

    2-head record → playback loop

    1. Prepare. Clean and demagnetise the heads, check azimuth, and switch Dolby NR and any MPX filter off. Load a blank tape of the type selected above.
    2. Record the tone pass. Press Play test tones, put the deck into record on the line input, and record about 15–20 seconds. Set the record level so the tones sit well below 0 VU.
    3. Rewind. Stop recording, then rewind to the start of the tone pass.
    4. Playback. Play the tape back through the line out and press Start bias measurement.
    5. Compare the 10 kHz level against the low tone. A negative reading means the highs are low, so the tape is over-biased. A positive reading means the highs are high, so it is under-biased. Aim for inside your tolerance on both channels.
    6. Adjust the internal trim pots. Change the bias trimmer for that channel and tape type by about 1/8 of a turn, and note which way you turned it. Record a fresh section, rewind, play it back and compare again. Repeat until both channels are in tolerance.

    On a 2-head deck you cannot hear the tape while recording, so every adjustment needs a new record, rewind and playback pass. A 3-head deck with tape monitor can read the tones off the tape live while recording, so there you can turn the pot as you watch the gauges. A large high-frequency loss that bias does not fix usually means dirty or misaligned heads.
